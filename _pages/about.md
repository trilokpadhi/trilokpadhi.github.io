I am a PhD student in Computer Science at [Georgia State University](https://www.gsu.edu/), advised by [Prof. Ugur Kursuncu](https://www.ugurkursuncu.com/) in the [SWAN AI Research Group](https://www.ugurkursuncu.com/SWAN-AI/). I also work closely with Prof. Yi Ding, Prof. Valerie Shalin, and Dr. Yaman Kumar Singla.

My research is about making multimodal models and agents **interpretable and trustworthy**. Large vision-language models can describe an image and act on the world, but they are confidently wrong in ways we cannot yet predict, and they fail partway through long tasks without signalling it. Three threads of my work push against this:

- **Interpretability and calibration.** Reading the internal state of an LLM agent step by step, so failure becomes detectable before the trajectory ends, and grounding a model's stated confidence in the visual evidence it can actually point to.
- **Knowledge-enhanced multimodal learning.** Infusing commonsense and structured knowledge into compact vision-language models, so small models can reason about context that neither the image nor the caption states outright.
- **Safety and social impact.** Red-teaming vision-language models and agents under realistic, multi-turn adversarial pressure — harassment, toxicity, manipulation — and studying how LLMs behave in sensitive settings such as anxiety support.

Before and during my PhD I have been an Applied Scientist Intern at **Siemens GenAI R&D**, a Research Intern at **SRI International** (Neuro-Symbolic Computing and Intelligence group), and a research intern at **Bosch Research** and **NVIDIA Research**. I also spent a year as a Data Scientist at **Rakuten** in Bangalore.

I am always glad to talk about multimodal reasoning, agent interpretability, and AI safety — feel free to [get in touch](mailto:tpadhi1@student.gsu.edu).
